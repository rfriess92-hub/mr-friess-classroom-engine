## Scientific-currency boundaries

- Attribution instruction begins with a neutral fictional case. Do not use a real death or contested racialized case as the opener before analytic norms are established.
- Fundamental attribution error is a research concept about attribution patterns, not a license to infer motives or diagnose an individual from sparse evidence.
- Classic conformity and obedience studies are used for concepts, historical evidence, methods, and ethics. Do not turn one classic study into a universal claim about what any person or group will do.
- Stereotype, prejudice, and discrimination are kept conceptually distinct. Systems/context can shape outcomes without reducing every unequal outcome to one individual attitude.
- Eyewitness accuracy is procedure-sensitive, not uniformly poor or uniformly reliable.
- Leading-question research supports bounded conclusions about how wording can influence reports; it does not prove that memory is always false or that a witness is inherently unreliable.
- Wrongful convictions can have multiple interacting contributors. Do not imply one eyewitness factor explains every case or transfer a U.S. statistic into a Canadian claim without qualification.
- Confidence is interpreted in relation to timing and procedure. Later confidence inflation is not treated as equivalent to a properly captured initial confidence statement.
- Identification procedures should reduce avoidable contamination and administrator influence; safeguards improve evidence quality without making identification infallible.

## Student-safety / disclosure boundary

No Unit 6 task requires students to disclose personal experiences of discrimination, violence, crime, victimization, police/court involvement, political identity, prejudice, or private beliefs. Use fictional, historical, public, or population-level material. Classroom conformity or leadership tasks are demonstrations of a procedure, not measures of student character.
