## Scientific-currency boundaries

- Mood-disorder biology is taught as interacting genetic, neural, endocrine, developmental, psychological, and social influences; avoid one-way biological-cause language.
- Retire simple neurotransmitter/“chemical imbalance” explanations. Neurotransmitters are components of interacting systems, not a complete single-cause account.
- Canadian prevalence claims name year, population, measure, and denominator. Survey estimates are not identical to clinician diagnosis and categories can overlap.
- Canadian access is framed as a system problem involving availability, navigation, affordability, waiting, geography, accessibility, culture/language, and stigma rather than an insurance-first U.S. model.
- Treatment is presented as evidence-based options whose fit depends on condition, severity, person, preferences, access, safety, and context; no one approach is treated as universally best.
- Cultural safety/humility uses FNHA as a teacher context anchor. Do not homogenize First Nations or invent local Nation-specific protocols.
- Suicide/self-harm material is not a graded student diagnosis activity. If used, it remains teacher-led, method-free, current, and support-oriented.

## Student-safety / disclosure boundary

No Unit 5 task requires students to disclose diagnoses, symptoms, treatment history, suicide/self-harm history, substance-use history, trauma, family mental-health history, sexuality, identity, or other private health information. Students are not asked to diagnose themselves or peers, role-play disorders, or complete clinical screening instruments.

## Security boundary

The Unit 5 Test remains outside this public source map. Secure items, answer keys, test-bank locators, accommodations, and student data remain in teacher-controlled local/school storage. Unit 5 creates no new controlled-release family.
